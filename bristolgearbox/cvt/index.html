What is a CVT gearbox?

CVT stands for Continuously Variable Transmission, a type of single-speed automatic gearbox. Instead of a set of fixed gears, it uses two variable pulleys linked by a steel belt or chain, so the ratio changes smoothly and the engine can stay at its most efficient speed. CVTs are used in many different cars, but they are most often found in smaller ones: their compact size and simple construction make them more cost-effective there than a conventional automatic. Toyota, Nissan and Honda all use CVTs, and Audi’s Multitronic is a variation of the design.

In many ways a CVT drives like a regular automatic. There’s no clutch pedal, so you can drive one on an automatic-only UK licence. And like a regular automatic, a CVT version of a car usually costs more than the manual.

Common CVT problems

  • Whining or droning noise
  • Juddering or shuddering when accelerating
  • Revs rising without the car speeding up
  • Delay when selecting Drive or Reverse
  • Overheating warnings or limp mode
  • Fluid leaks

CVT diagnostics and servicing

CVTs are sensitive to the condition and type of their fluid, so using the correct manufacturer-spec fluid matters. We diagnose CVT faults with Bosch equipment that reads the transmission controller directly, then explain what we found and what it will cost to put right.
